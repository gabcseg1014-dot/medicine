How it Started

One of the first pieces of evidence is from 2600 BC when the Egyptian Imhotep (Chancellor to King Djoser) described the diagnosis and treatment of about 200 diseases. In ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia, people wore protective amulets as a way to protect themselves from illness. After his discovery, Imhotep was worshipped as a God of Medicine.

We can observe how, early in time, people often turned to God to heal them. In Ancient Rome, people who were sick or hurt were expected to visit the temple of a healing God and would even buy a clay version of the body part that hurt them, often referred to as terracotta votives.

In those times, people believed that the healing God would visit them in their dreams and tell them the solution to their pain, which could vary from visiting the local medical practitioner for healing salve to making sacrifices.

Further Advances

As time went on, more advances were made to both medicine and its related technology. In around 950 BC, the world's oldest prosthetic devices were made in Egypt. These were wooden and leather toes. Later on in 300-500 BC, both Greeks and Romans created new surgical tools that would go on to inspire modern ones. These tools were made from bronze.

The next crucial moment for the evolution of medicine can be seen in 1249 when Roger Bacon invented the spectacles.
